The story

How a skipping record broke design

By 1980 Ettore Sottsass was 63, a respected designer of Olivetti typewriters and a veteran of Italy's radical-design scene. He was also bored. Modernism had become a polite corporate beige, and "good design" meant chrome and restraint.

On 11 December 1980 he invited a group of younger designers to his Milan flat. Bob Dylan's Stuck Inside of Mobile with the Memphis Blues Again played over and over, the needle stuck. By the end of the night the group had a name that meant both the blues city of Tennessee and the ancient capital of Egypt — and a plan to make furniture that looked like nothing anyone had seen.

Nine months later, on 18 September 1981, the first Memphis collection opened at the Arc '74 showroom in Milan. More than two thousand people crammed the street. Critics called it vulgar, childish and brilliant, often in the same review.

  • 1980The Dylan night. Sottsass, De Lucchi, Du Pasquier, Sowden, Bedin, Zanini, Cibic, Thun and friends found the group.
  • 1981First show. The Carlton room divider, Casablanca sideboard and Super lamp debut.
  • 1982Critic Barbara Radice's writing gives the movement a voice; the collective shows in Paris, London, Tokyo.
  • 1985Sottsass steps away; he later compared Memphis to an intense love affair — wonderful, and never meant to last.
  • 1987The group dissolves. The pieces go to museums — and Karl Lagerfeld fills his Monte Carlo flat with them.
  • 2016David Bowie's Memphis collection sells at Sotheby's for over a million pounds. The squiggles are back.

The (anti-)rules

Six ways to annoy a modernist

1

Laminate is noble

Cheap plastic laminate from bars and diners, printed with bacteria and sponge patterns, applied to luxury furniture. Materials have no class.

2

Pattern before form

Surface decoration is not a crime. It is the point. Nathalie Du Pasquier and George Sowden treated every plane as a canvas.

3

Clash the colours

Pink against red, mint against black, yellow against lilac. Harmony is optional; energy is required.

4

Primitive shapes

Cylinders, cones, spheres, zigzags — stacked like a child's blocks or an Aztec totem. Geometry as toy.

5

Function is negotiable

The Carlton's shelves slant. Who cares? An object's job is also to make you feel something.

6

Pop is culture

Suburbia, Hollywood, comics, kitsch and the ancient world, all in one room. High and low, together at last.
