墨流し · floating ink

Since the twelfth century, marblers in Japan have touched a brush of sumi to still water, then a brush of clear water beside it, again and again, until rings nest like the growth of a tree.

A breath, a fan or a single hair then draws the rings into currents. The paper is laid on the surface and lifted: one print, never repeated.

Here the water obeys the Navier–Stokes equations. Every drop pushes the older rings outward, preserving their area exactly.
