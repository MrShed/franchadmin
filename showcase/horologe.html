One hundred and eighty-six parts, finished by hand in a former dairy above Le Brassus. It is keeping your time now.

Maison fondée 2009

VERRIER

Independent watchmakers. Eleven pieces a year.

I

The plate is cut away until only intention remains

Each mainplate begins as a disc of German silver 1.8 millimetres thick. Over nine days, Élodie Verrier removes nearly seventy percent of it with a jeweller's saw — by hand, at a bench lit from the north.

What is left is the minimum needed to hold the train in alignment, and not a gram more. The bridges are then frosted, bevelled at forty-five degrees and polished with gentian wood until the angle throws a single unbroken line of light.

II

An escapement that speaks in fifths of a second

The Swiss lever escapement releases the train five times each second. Listen closely to a V-01 and you will hear it: a soft, even patter, 18,000 times an hour — slower than modern movements, chosen for its warmer beat and longer life.

The escape wheel's twenty teeth are cut in steel, then each one is polished on a boxwood lap. It takes an afternoon. It will run for a century.

III

Rose gold, alloyed in the workshop

Our 5N gold is poured in-house from gold, copper and a trace of palladium, which keeps its blush from darkening. The colour is warmer than commercial rose gold; the gear teeth, cut from it, catch light like the inside of a shell.

IV

Assembled, regulated, then left to listen

After assembly each movement rests in a timing cabinet for thirty days, turned through six positions and three temperatures. We regulate to within two seconds a day, then engrave the name of the person who did it beneath the balance cock.
