02

A short history of the neutral voice

The style grew out of the Kunstgewerbeschulen of Basel and Zürich in the years after the Second World War. Its roots run back through Jan Tschichold's Die neue Typographie (1928) and the Bauhaus, but Swiss designers turned that avant-garde manifesto into a calm, repeatable method.

A neutral country, three official languages, and a booming export economy made a legible, language-light visual system not only an ideal but a necessity. By the 1960s the corporate world — airlines, chemical giants, transit systems — had adopted it wholesale.

  1. 1928Jan Tschichold publishes Die neue Typographie, arguing for asymmetric layouts and sans-serif type.
  2. 1947Armin Hofmann begins teaching at the Allgemeine Gewerbeschule Basel; Emil Ruder joins him the following year.
  3. 1955Josef Müller-Brockmann's Musica Viva posters for the Tonhalle Zürich bring abstract geometry to concert publicity.
  4. 1957Max Miedinger and Eduard Hoffmann release Neue Haas Grotesk — later renamed Helvetica. Adrian Frutiger's Univers appears the same year.
  5. 1958Neue Grafik magazine launches, edited by Müller-Brockmann, Lohse, Neuburg and Vivarelli — the movement's house journal.
  6. 1961Karl Gerstner's Designing Programmes proposes design as a system of rules, decades before generative design.
  7. 1967Emil Ruder publishes Typographie; Massimo Vignelli designs the New York subway signage in the Swiss manner.
  8. 1981Müller-Brockmann's Grid Systems in Graphic Design is published. Meanwhile Wolfgang Weingart, in Basel, is already breaking it apart.
03

Personen

Teachers more than stars. Most of the style's influence spread through classrooms in Basel and Zürich and the students who carried it to Yale, Ulm and New York.

04

Eight working principles

Not rules for their own sake — each one removes a decision the reader would otherwise have to make.

1

The grid is the argument

A modular grid decides sizes, margins and positions before any content arrives. Order becomes visible and consistent across a whole series.

2

Asymmetry, not centring

Centred axes are static. Asymmetric balance creates tension and leads the eye through the page.

3

Sans serif grotesk

Akzidenz-Grotesk, then Helvetica and Univers: faces without historical costume, "objective" letterforms for an objective message.

4

Flush left, ragged right

Even word spacing matters more than a straight right edge. Justified text is abandoned.

5

Photography over illustration

The camera is trusted as a neutral witness; drawings are reserved for pure geometry.

6

Negative space works

White is not left over. It is a structural material, as deliberate as any line of type.

7

Scale contrast

One very large element, many small ones. Hierarchy is built with size and weight, never ornament.

8

Content first

"The designer's job is to make the message clear" — the rest is discipline.
