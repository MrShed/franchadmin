浮世絵版

Pictures of the floating world

For two and a half centuries, the cheapest pictures in Edo — sold for about the price of a double serving of noodles — were printed from carved cherry wood. Courtesans, kabuki actors, sumo wrestlers, ghosts and, finally, landscapes: ukiyo-e showed the pleasures of a world that knew it was passing.

歴史

A brief history

Ukiyo once meant "sorrowful world", a Buddhist idea of life's impermanence. In the peace of the Edo period (1603–1868), a pun turned it into the "floating world": the theatres, teahouses and pleasure quarters of a booming merchant class, where life was to be enjoyed precisely because it wouldn't last.

Painters could only make one picture at a time; woodblock printing could make hundreds. Publishers commissioned designs, and a team of specialists — artist, carver and printer — turned them into affordable prints for the public.

At first prints were black ink, coloured by hand. In 1765 Suzuki Harunobu led the invention of full-colour nishiki-e ("brocade pictures") using a registration mark, the kentō, to align many blocks precisely.

A new imported pigment, Prussian blue (bero-ai), became affordable in the late 1820s. It didn't fade like plant indigo, and it made possible the landscape boom of the 1830s — Hokusai's Fuji series and Hiroshige's roads — that the West would later fall in love with.

絵師

Two masters of landscape

北斎

Katsushika Hokusai

1760–1849 · used more than thirty names in his life

Restless, prolific and funny, Hokusai moved house more than ninety times and styled himself "the old man mad about drawing". He published the Hokusai Manga sketchbooks, and in his early seventies designed the Fuji series that made him immortal.

  • Under the Wave off Kanagawa (c. 1831)
  • Fine Wind, Clear Morning — "Red Fuji"
  • One Hundred Views of Mount Fuji (1834)
広重

Utagawa Hiroshige

1797–1858 · a fire warden's son from Edo

Where Hokusai is dramatic, Hiroshige is atmospheric: rain, snow, mist, moonlight, travellers on the road. His Tōkaidō series made him a star, and his daring cropped compositions were copied stroke for stroke by Van Gogh.

  • Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō (1833–34)
  • One Hundred Famous Views of Edo (1856–59)
  • Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge
Suzuki Harunobu鈴木春信

c. 1725–1770. Pioneer of full-colour printing; delicate, dreamlike young lovers.

Kitagawa Utamaro喜多川歌麿

c. 1753–1806. Close-up portraits of women, sensitive and psychologically alive.

Tōshūsai Sharaku東洲斎写楽

Active 1794–95. Ten months of ferocious kabuki portraits, then vanished.

Utagawa Kuniyoshi歌川国芳

1798–1861. Warriors, giant skeletons and cats — the wild imagination of late Edo.

    美学

    What the West learned from the floating world

    線

    The outline

    A confident key line carved in wood, holding flat areas of colour — no modelling, no shadow.

    平

    Flat colour

    Colour as shape, not as light. It shaped the posters of Toulouse-Lautrec and the Nabis.

    暈

    Bokashi

    A gradient wiped by hand onto a wet block, so skies and seas fade from deep to pale.

    間

    Asymmetry and ma

    Off-centre subjects, empty space that breathes, a tiny Fuji balanced against a giant wave.

    切

    Daring crops

    Things cut by the frame, views through branches, a plum tree trunk filling the foreground.

    今

    The everyday

    Travellers, fishermen, actors and weather — ordinary life as worthy subject for art.
