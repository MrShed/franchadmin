I. On the Guild

The mushroom is only the fruit. The organism is the thread — kilometres of it under a single oak, trading sugar for phosphorus, stitching the forest into one slow body.

We are a society of amateurs in the old sense: people who love a thing. Our members include a retired anaesthetist, three schoolteachers, a baker who makes the best chanterelle toast in the county and a dozen children who can tell a Russula from a Lactarius by the milk. We meet each Saturday of the season at the Chapter House and walk the wood in small parties, each led by a Warden with at least seven years' standing.

Nothing is picked that won't be identified, and nothing identified is eaten without a second Warden's word.

  1. Rule the FirstNever eat a mushroom identified by one person, one book, or one app.
  2. Rule the SecondTake a spore print. The colour of the dust is older and more honest than the colour of the cap.
  3. Rule the ThirdCut, don't pull. Leave the smallest and the oldest. Carry a basket, never a bag.
