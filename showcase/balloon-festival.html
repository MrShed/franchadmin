Wake up early. It's worth it.

At 5:40 in the morning the Dawn Patrol goes up alone — eleven balloons lit from inside, glowing like paper lanterns against the last of the stars. By seven, the field is a riot: pilots shouting, crews hauling envelopes out of trailers, the roar of burners stacking on top of each other until you feel it in your ribs.

The Fiesta happens because of the "Caliche Box," a quirk of our valley's winds. Low air flows south, upper air flows north, so a pilot can drift out, climb, and ride back to land within sight of where they started. That little loop of weather built a whole town's worth of tradition.

Bring a jacket, a thermos, and somebody you like. We'll handle the sunrise.
