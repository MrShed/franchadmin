    千羽鶴 · SENBAZURUThe legend of a thousand cranes

    The crane, tsuru, was said to live for a thousand years. An old belief holds that anyone who folds a thousand of them, strung together on thread, will be granted a wish: recovery from illness, a long marriage, a safe return.

    In 1955 Sadako Sasaki, twelve years old and ill with leukaemia a decade after the bombing of Hiroshima, began folding cranes from medicine wrappers and scraps of gift paper in her hospital bed. Her classmates kept folding after she died that October, and raised money for a statue of a girl holding a golden crane.

    This is our cry. This is our prayer. Peace in the world.

    Those words are carved beneath her statue in Hiroshima's Peace Memorial Park, where strings of cranes arrive from schools around the world every year, many millions of them, so many that the city now recycles the paper into new things.
